Instagram & Gen Z

I chose Instagram to represent the zeitgeist of my generation , a social media platform launched in 2010. Originally designed for sharing everyday photos, Instagram has evolved into a platform centered around self-presentation, identity construction, and social validation.

As one of the most widely used platforms among Gen Z, Instagram reflects how communication, identity, and social interaction have shifted into digital spaces. As of the 2020s, Instagram has over 2 billion active users, showcasing its global influence on culture and behaviour.

Rather than simply capturing life, Instagram now shapes how life is experienced and the standards of life. Users carefully select what they post, creating curated versions of themselves for others to see. This shows how identity is no longer just personal, but something that is continuously constructed and shared online.

Instagram represents more than just technology - it reflects how an entire generation experiences social life, identity, and validation through a digital lens.

Representation of the Zeitgeist

Instagram represents a generation defined by constant visibility, connection, and performance.

Gen Z spends a significant amount of time on social media, using platforms like Instagram daily for communication, entertainment, and self-expression. Unlike earlier generations, identity is no longer private - it is shared, viewed, and judged all in real time.

Features such as likes, followers, stories, and the explore page encourage users to seek attention and recognition. The rise of influencer culture also shows how individuals can build entire careers based on online identities.

“Life is not just lived, it is displayed.”
This reflects a cultural shift where being seen, recognized, and validated by others has become a central part of social existence for Gen Z.

Generational Beliefs

One key belief of this generation is that identity is constructed rather than fixed.

Instagram allows users to carefully choose what they share, creating a version of themselves that may not fully reflect reality. This shows how identity can be shaped and edited depending on how individuals want to be perceived.

Another belief is that social validation determines value, where likes, comments, and followers act as indicators of success and popularity.

Overall, this reflects a broader shift where identity is socially constructed and continuously shaped through interaction, statistics and feedback from others.

Critical Insight

Although Instagram promotes creativity and connection, it also encourages comparison and unrealistic standards.

Users are constantly exposed to carefully edited, filtered, and idealised content, which can distort their perception of reality and create pressure to meet unrealistic expectations.

“What we see online is often a highlight, not the full picture.”
This creates a contradiction within the generation, where individuals value being authentic but still feel pressure to present a perfect and curated version of themselves.

Mental Health Impact

Instagram has a significant impact on mental health, particularly through comparison, pressure, and self-doubt.

Research has shown that increased social media use is associated with higher levels of anxiety, lower self-esteem, and increased social comparison among teens.

“Everyone ELSE seems happier, more successful, and more confident.”
While Instagram connects people globally, it can also highlight feelings of insecurity and pressure to meet unrealistic standards of success and appearance.

The 2020s Shift

In the 2020s, there has been a noticeable shift toward authenticity and mental health awareness.

Many Gen Z users now recognise the negative effects of social media and actively try to limit usage or engage or create more genuine and unfiltered content.

For example, trends such as “photo dumps,” less edited photos, and open discussions about mental health reflect a movement toward being more real online.

However, even authenticity can become performative, as users still seek validation through likes and engagement.

This shows that while the way people present themselves has changed, the underlying need for visibility, approval, and social validation has remained consistent over time.

Connection. This is my own external research that I completed.

Instagram can be connected to the sociological concept of the Looking Glass Self by Charles Horton Cooley.

This theory suggests that people develop their identity based on how they believe others perceive or see them.

On Instagram, likes, comments, and followers give constant feedback, shaping how users see themselves and how they choose to present their identity online.

This demonstrates how Instagram reinforces the Looking Glass Self, as people continuously adjust their behaviour based on perceived judgments, creating a loop where identity is shaped through social interaction.
